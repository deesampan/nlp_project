How it works

  1. Collect the reviews. 1.3 million review entries from the dataset, reduced to 134,612 real ones after removing duplicates and junk.
  2. Split each review into short pieces. A review often praises one dish and criticises another, so we break it at sentences and at words like but.
  3. Find the dish names. We match each piece against a dictionary of 2,774 dish names built from the dataset itself.
  4. Decide good or bad. A trained model reads each piece and decides whether it is positive or negative, and that verdict counts only for the dish named in it.
  5. Add it up. Each dish gets a rating: the share of its comments that were positive. Dishes mentioned fewer than 5 times are left out.

How accurate is it? Tested on 26,923 reviews it had never seen, the model tells a positive comment from a negative one correctly 96.5% of the time.
